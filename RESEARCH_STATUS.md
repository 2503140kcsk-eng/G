# Research status and formalization record

**Snapshot date:** 2026-11-11 (JST)  
**Question:** Is (e+pi) transcendental?

## 1. Mathematical status

**Unconditional status: open.** The sources checked for this snapshot do not establish even that (e+pi) is irrational. Therefore they do not establish its transcendence. This is a statement about the literature checked, not a proof that no proof can exist anywhere.

A directly relevant 2026 preprint is Runlong Yu, [*Tail Criteria, No-Go Audits, and Apéry-Type Certificate Obstructions for the Irrationality of (e+pi)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17303), arXiv:2606.17303v1, submitted 15 June 2026. It derives necessary arithmetic/tail conditions under a rationality assumption and audits several bounded families of candidate certificates. It explicitly does not produce a certificate or prove a universal impossibility theorem. It is a preprint, not evidence of a completed proof.

Consequently, the repository does **not** claim an unconditional theorem `Transcendental ℚ ePlusPi`.

## 2. Established mathematics and exact implication boundary

Known classical results:

- (e) is transcendental (Hermite).
- (pi) is transcendental (Lindemann).
- (e^pi) is transcendental (Gelfond–Schneider).

These statements do not imply that (e+pi) is transcendental.

An unconditional consequence is:

[
oxed{(e+pi)	ext{ is transcendental}quadlorquad(epi)	ext{ is transcendental}.}
]

To prove it, suppose (s=e+pi) and (p=epi) are both algebraic. Then (e) and (pi) are roots of (X^2-sX+p). Algebraicity is transitive through algebraic coefficient extensions, so both roots would be algebraic over (mathbb Q), contradicting the classical theorems. The disjunction does not say which of the two is transcendental.

### Schanuel's conjecture: a conditional route

Schanuel's conjecture is the assertion that for any finite (mathbb Q)-linearly independent family (z_1,ldots,z_n) of complex numbers,

[
operatorname{trdeg}_{mathbb Q}
mathbb Q(z_1,ldots,z_n,e^{z_1},ldots,e^{z_n})ge n.
]

Set (z_1=1) and (z_2=ipi). They are linearly independent over (mathbb Q), since their real and imaginary parts force both rational coefficients to vanish. Euler's identity gives (e^{ipi}=-1), while (e^1=e). Thus Schanuel gives transcendence degree at least two for (mathbb Q(e,ipi)). Since it is generated by two elements, the degree is exactly two, so (e) and (ipi) are algebraically independent. Because (mathbb Q(i)/mathbb Q) is algebraic, (e) and (pi) are algebraically independent as well.

If (e+pi) were algebraic, its minimal polynomial would give a nonzero polynomial relation between (e) and (pi), a contradiction. Thus:

[
oxed{	ext{Schanuel's conjecture} Longrightarrow       e+pi	ext{ is transcendental}.}
]

This implication is conditional. Schanuel's conjecture is not proved and is not introduced as an axiom in this project.

## 3. Lean formalization status

The feature branch `formalize-algebraic-number-closure` contains these completed developments:

### 3.1 Individual transcendence

- `lean/LindemannAlgebraicPart.lean`: algebraic part of the Lindemann–Weierstrass argument, adapted with the analytic estimates from Mathlib's `Mathlib.NumberTheory.Transcendental.Lindemann.AnalyticalPart`.
- `lean/LindemannBasic.lean`: proves `Complex.linearIndependent_exp`, `Complex.algebraicIndependent_exp`, `Complex.transcendental_exp`, `Complex.transcendental_e`, and `Real.transcendental_pi`.
- `lean/HermiteLindemannBridge.lean`: derives the rational-base statements `HermiteLindemannBridge.transcendental_e` and `HermiteLindemannBridge.transcendental_pi`, together with the general algebraic-exponent bridge.

The proof source is included in the project and is kernel-checked under the pinned Mathlib environment. The existence of an analytic estimate in Mathlib alone must not be confused with a completed Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem: the algebraic argument is also part of the project development.

### 3.2 Algebraic closure and unconditional disjunction

- `lean/AlgebraicClosure.lean`: closure of algebraic elements under addition and multiplication; algebraicity of roots of nonzero polynomials with algebraic coefficients; an algebraic-extension descent lemma.
- `lean/EPlusPi.lean`: defines `ePlusPi`, `eTimesPi`, states the open target as `EPlusPiTranscendental`, and proves `e_and_pi_transcendental` and `e_add_pi_or_mul_transcendental`.

### 3.3 Conditional Schanuel module

- `lean/SchanuelConditional.lean`: formalizes `SchanuelConjecture` as a proposition, proves the linear independence of (1,ipi), specializes the conjecture to show algebraic independence of (e,ipi), and proves `SchanuelConditional.transcendental_ePlusPi_of_schanuel`.
- This theorem takes a proof of `SchanuelConjecture` as an explicit argument. There is no project axiom asserting Schanuel's conjecture.
- The proof uses the exact finite transcendence-basis API and the fraction-field relationship between the field adjoined by a pair and its generated subalgebra; it does not postulate the resulting algebraic independence.

### 3.4 What remains unproved

The unconditional proposition `EPlusPiTranscendental` remains open. There is no theorem declaration that proves it, no custom axiom, and no `sorry`/ `admit` used to fill the gap. A successful proof of it would in particular prove the irrationality of (e+pi), which the literature checked still regards as open.

## 5. Open research directions (not claimed proofs)

1. Derive a new effective irrationality measure for (e+pi), or construct nonzero integer linear forms (A_n(e+pi)+B_n) whose arithmetic denominator bounds and analytic decay jointly force a contradiction.
2. Investigate mixed Hermite–Padé approximations with both (e) and (pi). The 2026 preprint rules out or audits only specified bounded families; it does not exclude every possible construction.
3. Search for a genuinely new relation between (e) and (pi) that is stronger than the current individual transcendence results but does not assume Schanuel.
4. Keep all conditional implications separated from unconditional theorem declarations in Lean.

The decisive missing object is an unconditional argument proving that (e+pi
otinoverline{mathbb Q}), or even (e+pi
otinmathbb Q). No computational search over finite examples can supply that universal proof by itself.
