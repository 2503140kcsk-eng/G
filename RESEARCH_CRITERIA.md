# Target-specific transcendence criteria for \(e+\pi\)

**Research note:** 2026-11-12  
**Branch:** formalize-algebraic-number-closure  
**Status:** proved conditional criteria; the required approximation family is not known.

This note separates three questions that must not be conflated:

1. algebraic independence of \(e\) and \(\pi\);
2. transcendence of \(e+\pi\);
3. irrationality of \(e+\pi\).

The current unconditional target in this repository is (2). Even (3) is open in the literature checked through this date. Nothing below claims an unconditional solution.

## 1. A one-instance Schanuel hypothesis

Write \(\mathrm{SC}_{1,i\pi}\) for the following single instance of Schanuel's conjecture:

\[
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(e,i\pi)\geq 2.
\]

This follows from the full Schanuel conjecture by applying it to \(z_1=1\) and \(z_2=i\pi\). These inputs are \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent, and their exponentials are \(e\) and \(-1\). Since \(1,-1\) are algebraic, the field generated by the inputs and their exponentials is exactly \(\mathbb Q(e,i\pi)\).

Because the field is generated by two elements, the inequality above is equivalent to algebraic independence of \(e\) and \(i\pi\). Since \(i\) is algebraic and nonzero, it is equivalent to algebraic independence of \(e\) and \(\pi\). In particular it implies that \(e+\pi\) is transcendental.

**Logical status.** This is weaker in *scope of quantification* than full Schanuel: it asks for only one fixed input pair instead of every finite \(\mathbb Q\)-linearly independent family. It is not a new theorem, and a strict logical separation between this instance and full Schanuel is not known here. The Lean development in *lean/SchanuelConditional.lean* already proves the implication from the full conjecture; this note does not present the instance as an unconditional fact.

## 2. A monomial-form criterion for algebraic independence of \(e,\pi\)

For \(D\geq 1\), let

\[
\mathcal M_D=\{e^i\pi^j:i,j\in\mathbb N,\ 1\leq i+j\leq D\},
\qquad
m_D=|\mathcal M_D|={D+2\choose 2}-1.
\]

Suppose that for every \(D\geq1\) there are constants \(c_{1,D},c_{2,D}>0\), positive exponents \(\tau_{1,D},\tau_{2,D}\), and a nondecreasing sequence \(\sigma_D(n)\) with

\[
\sigma_D(n)\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
\frac{\sigma_D(n+1)}{\sigma_D(n)}\longrightarrow1,
\]

such that for every sufficiently large \(n\) there is an integer linear form

\[
L_{D,n}=a_{00,D,n}
+\sum_{1\leq i+j\leq D}a_{ij,D,n}e^i\pi^j
\]

satisfying

\[
\sum_{i+j\leq D}|a_{ij,D,n}|\leq e^{\sigma_D(n)}
\]

and

\[
c_{1,D}e^{-\tau_{1,D}\sigma_D(n)}
\leq |L_{D,n}|
\leq c_{2,D}e^{-\tau_{2,D}\sigma_D(n)},
\]

with

\[
\frac{1+\tau_{1,D}}{1+\tau_{1,D}-\tau_{2,D}}>m_D.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then \(e\) and \(\pi\) are algebraically independent over \(\mathbb Q\).

**Proof.** Fix \(D\). Apply Nesterenko's linear-independence criterion to the \(m_D\) real numbers in \(\mathcal M_D\). The coefficient-height estimate, the two-sided value estimate, and the regular-growth condition on \(\sigma_D\) are exactly its hypotheses. It gives

\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
\bigl(\{1\}\cup\mathcal M_D\bigr)
\geq
\frac{1+\tau_{1,D}}{1+\tau_{1,D}-\tau_{2,D}}
>m_D.
\]

The span contains \(m_D+1\) displayed numbers, so its dimension is exactly \(m_D+1\); hence those numbers are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\). If a nonzero polynomial \(P\in\mathbb Q[X,Y]\) vanished at \((e,\pi)\), choose \(D\) at least its total degree and clear its rational coefficient denominators. This would give a nontrivial rational linear relation among \(\{1\}\cup\mathcal M_D\), a contradiction. Therefore no such polynomial exists. \(\square\)

This is a **specialization of an existing criterion**, not a newly proved transcendence theorem. Its research content would lie in constructing the forms \(L_{D,n}\) for the actual pair \((e,\pi)\) with the stated exponent inequality. No such family is known here.

A precise asymptotic estimate
\[
|L_{D,n}|=\exp\bigl(-\tau_D\sigma_D(n)+o(\sigma_D(n))\bigr)
\]
**does** imply the two-sided hypotheses of Nesterenko's criterion with a small exponent margin. For any sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), eventually
\[
e^{-(\tau_D+\varepsilon)\sigma_D(n)}
\leq |L_{D,n}|
\leq e^{-(\tau_D-\varepsilon)\sigma_D(n)}.
\]
Thus take \(\tau_{1,D}=\tau_D+\varepsilon\) and \(\tau_{2,D}=\tau_D-\varepsilon\). The resulting dimension lower bound is
\[
\frac{1+\tau_D+\varepsilon}{1+2\varepsilon}
\longrightarrow 1+\tau_D
\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).
\]
Consequently, if \(\tau_D>m_D-1\), choose
\[
0<\varepsilon<
\min\!\left(\tau_D,\frac{\tau_D-(m_D-1)}{2m_D-1}\right)
\]
to make (2.1) hold. For example, when \(D=1\), \(m_D=2\), and \(\tau_D=9/5\), choosing \(\varepsilon=1/5\) gives \((\tau_{1,D},\tau_{2,D})=(2,8/5)\) and the lower bound \(15/7>2\).

The distinction is that one must not literally substitute \(\tau_{1,D}=\tau_{2,D}=\tau_D\) into the fixed-exponent formula: the asymptotic \(o(\sigma_D(n))\) term permits bracketing by \(\tau_D\pm\varepsilon\), and the limiting criterion is obtained only after taking arbitrarily small positive \(\varepsilon\). Since \(m_D={D+2\choose2}-1\), the required asymptotic decay rate \(\tau_D\) grows quadratically with \(D\). This remains a conditional criterion: constructing such a family for the actual monomials \(e^i\pi^j\) is the unresolved part.
## 3. A primitive denominator-survival criterion for \(e+\pi\)

Put \(\alpha=e+\pi\). Let \(a_n,b_n\in\mathbb Q\), and let \(D_n\geq1\) be a common denominator, so that

\[
A_n=D_na_n\in\mathbb Z,\qquad B_n=D_nb_n\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Assume \((A_n,B_n)\neq(0,0)\), put

\[
g_n=\gcd(|A_n|,|B_n|),\quad
A_n^\circ=A_n/g_n,\quad B_n^\circ=B_n/g_n,
\]

and define the primitive integer form and its height by

\[
L_n=A_n^\circ\alpha+B_n^\circ,\qquad
H_n=\max(2,|A_n^\circ|,|B_n^\circ|).
\]

Assume \(L_n\neq0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\) and \(H_n\to\infty\).

### Theorem 3.1 (primitive super-polynomial criterion)

If

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{-\log |L_n|}{\log H_n}=+\infty,
\tag{3.1}
\]

then \(\alpha=e+\pi\) is transcendental.

**Proof.** Suppose instead that \(\alpha\) is algebraic of degree \(d\geq1\). Liouville's inequality for a fixed algebraic number gives a constant \(c_\alpha>0\) such that, for coprime integers \(p,q\) with \(q\geq1\),

\[
\left|\alpha-\frac pq\right|\geq c_\alpha q^{-d}.
\]

For all sufficiently large \(n\), \(A_n^\circ\neq0\): otherwise primitiveness forces \(B_n^\circ=\pm1\), hence \(|L_n|=1\), contradicting (3.1). Apply the inequality to the reduced rational number $-B_n^\circ/A_n^\circ=p_n/q_n$, where $q_n=|A_n^\circ|$ and $p_n=-\operatorname{sgn}(A_n^\circ)B_n^\circ$. Then $p_n,q_n$ are coprime integers and $q_n\geq1$.

\[
|L_n|
=|A_n^\circ|
\left|\alpha+\frac{B_n^\circ}{A_n^\circ}\right|
\geq c_\alpha |A_n^\circ|^{1-d}
\geq c_\alpha H_n^{1-d}.
\]

But (3.1) says that for every fixed \(M>0\), eventually \(|L_n|\leq H_n^{-M}\). Choose \(M>d-1\). As \(H_n\to\infty\), this contradicts the lower bound \(c_\alpha H_n^{1-d}\). Thus \(\alpha\) is transcendental. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2 (irrationality certificate)

If \(L_n=A_n^\circ\alpha+B_n^\circ\) is a sequence of nonzero integer linear forms with \(L_n\to0\), then \(\alpha\) is irrational.

**Proof.** If \(\alpha=p/q\in\mathbb Q\), then \(qL_n\in\mathbb Z\). Since \(L_n\neq0\), \(|L_n|\geq1/q\), contradicting \(L_n\to0\). \(\square\)

The distinction is essential: \(L_n\to0\) proves irrationality; condition (3.1), which requires decay faster than every fixed power of the primitive height, proves transcendence. A merely exponentially small error relative to an exponentially growing denominator need not prove transcendence.

### 3.3 Explicit denominator-loss budget

Suppose an analytic construction first gives

\[
\Lambda_n=a_n\alpha+b_n,\qquad
|\Lambda_n|\leq e^{-\tau_n},
\]

with rational coefficients, common denominator \(D_n\), and raw integer height

\[
H_n^{\rm raw}=\max(2,|D_na_n|,|D_nb_n|).
\]

After denominator clearing and primitive reduction,

\[
|L_n|=\frac{D_n|\Lambda_n|}{g_n}\leq D_ne^{-\tau_n},
\qquad H_n\leq H_n^{\rm raw}.
\]

Consequently, a sufficient condition for (3.1) is

\[
\frac{\tau_n-\log D_n}{\log H_n^{\rm raw}}
\longrightarrow+\infty.
\tag{3.2}
\]

This ratio is the **surviving approximation exponent** used in this project: the numerator is analytic decay *after paying the denominator-clearing cost*, and the denominator is the logarithmic arithmetic height after primitive reduction. It is a convenient project-specific packaging of Liouville's inequality, not a claim of a new general theorem.

For irrationality alone, the weaker condition \(D_n|\Lambda_n|/g_n\to0\), with eventual non-vanishing, is sufficient.

## 4. The mixed integral source already in the literature

For \(P\in\mathbb Z[x]\), define the finite sum

\[
S_P(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\deg P}(-1)^jP^{(j)}(x),
\qquad A(P)=S_P(1).
\]

Then \(S_P'(x)+S_P(x)=P(x)\). Therefore

\[
\frac{d}{dx}\bigl(e^xS_P(x)\bigr)=e^xP(x),
\]

and

\[
\int_0^1P(x)e^x\,dx=eA(P)-S_P(0).
\]

Also,

\[
\int_0^1\frac{4A(P)}{1+x^2}\,dx
=4A(P)\arctan(1)=\pi A(P).
\]

Adding the two identities gives

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1\left(P(x)e^x+\frac{4A(P)}{1+x^2}\right)\,dx
=A(P)(e+\pi)-S_P(0).
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

Because \(P\) has integer coefficients, \(A(P)\) and \(S_P(0)\) are integers. Thus (4.1) gives exact integer linear forms in \(e+\pi\), with no initial denominator clearing. For each \(P\) with \((A(P),S_P(0))\neq(0,0)\), divide by their gcd before measuring height or error.

**Attribution boundary.** Identity (4.1) and the irrationality-certificate viewpoint are present in Runlong Yu, *Tail Criteria, No-Go Audits, and Apéry-Type Certificate Obstructions for the Irrationality of \(e+\pi\)*, arXiv:2606.17303v1 (2026). The identity is not claimed as a new discovery here. The additional criterion in §3 specifies what a primitive sequence from this or another construction would need to establish for *transcendence*, rather than merely irrationality.

## 5. What blocks an unconditional proof

The required construction is not supplied by (4.1). One must exhibit a sequence \(P_n\in\mathbb Z[x]\) for which, after gcd reduction,

\[
0<
\left|A(P_n)(e+\pi)-S_{P_n}(0)\right|/g_n
\]

has a logarithmic decay exponent divided by the logarithmic primitive height tending to \(+\infty\). The 2026 preprint audits several low-complexity families and a finite kernel-lattice search; its degree-14 example reduces by a common factor \(9\) to a continued-fraction convergent of \(e+\pi\). Those computations rule out only the tested constructions, not all possible constructions.

Thus the following are proved, but the decisive existence statement remains open:

- the conditional Schanuel implication already formalized in *lean/SchanuelConditional.lean*;
- the monomial-form implication in §2, by the published Nesterenko criterion;
- the primitive super-polynomial criterion in §3, by Liouville's inequality;
- the exact mixed integral identity in §4;
- no unconditional irrationality or transcendence proof for \(e+\pi\).

## 6. Formalization boundary

Two **general implications** from the approximation discussion are now Lean declarations in `lean/ApproximationCriteria.lean`, and are included in the `ProofAudit` dependency root:

1. `irrational_of_tendsto_zero_signed_integer_linear_forms`: if an integer-linear-form sequence is eventually nonzero and its absolute values tend to zero, then its target real number is irrational. The proof is the denominator-clearing contradiction: a hypothetical rational value would turn each form into a nonzero integer after multiplication by the fixed denominator.
2. `transcendental_of_superpolynomial_integer_linear_forms`: if the positive leading coefficients tend to infinity, the forms are eventually nonzero, and their absolute values are eventually smaller than every fixed inverse power of the coefficient height, then the target is a Liouville number and hence transcendental. This uses Mathlib's `Liouville.transcendental` theorem.

These are conditional **criteria**, not constructions of approximants for \(e+\pi\). The exact paper-level work still not formalized includes:

1. the primitive/gcd-normalized form of Theorem 3.1 with its logarithmic height-to-error limit (the Lean Liouville criterion above is a stronger, differently packaged sufficient hypothesis);
2. the full Nesterenko linear-independence criterion and its growth regularity hypotheses in Section 2;
3. the derivative-sum identity and mixed integral identity (4.1) in Section 4, including the integer-coefficient specialization;
4. an explicit sequence of nonzero integer forms for \(e+\pi\) whose values tend to zero (for irrationality), or meet the stronger super-polynomial condition (for transcendence).

The exact continuation and proof details are recorded in [MIXED_KERNEL_AUDIT_2026-11-14.md](MIXED_KERNEL_AUDIT_2026-11-14.md). Two points are important for future construction work:

- The degree-one polynomial (P(x)=(a-c)x+a) realizes arbitrary endpoint data ((A(P),C(P))=(a,c)). Hence the mixed integral identity does not by itself restrict the integer linear form; an additional degree-growing analytic structure is indispensable.
- For (P_n(x)=x^n(1-x)^n), the endpoint derivative coefficient satisfies (|A(P_n)|\ge (2n)!/2), while the exponential integral is at most (e(n!)^2/(2n+1)!). Therefore the associated form (A(P_n)(e+\pi)-C(P_n)) has absolute value tending to infinity, not zero.
